I'm an M.Tech student in Artificial Intelligence at the Indian Institute of Information Technology, Bhopal, working where distributed systems meet machine learning. My research is on federated learning: training one model across many independent, heterogeneous clients that never share their data, and keeping that training correct when some of them are faulty or malicious. I treat this as a distributed-systems problem as much as an ML one, with questions of Byzantine fault tolerance, replicated state, recovery and communication cost.

My current project, FedVC, borrows from version control to govern a federated system. The server's model lives in a Git-style repository with commits, per-client branches, checkpoint rollback and periodic verdicts on suspected clients, all around unmodified FedAvg. With 100 clients, 30 of them attackers, it cuts the poisoned share of aggregation weight 10–16× compared with nine robust-aggregation baselines, while uploading 18–24% less data. Earlier work on adaptive aggregation for blockchain-federated learning, published in Applied Intelligence, filters outlier updates in two stages before the aggregate is replicated across clients.

I also care about running models efficiently once they're trained. That has meant INT8 quantization and ONNX Runtime for fast CPU inference, and CUDA with multi-GPU and mixed-precision training. My experiments use multiple seeds, significance tests and leakage-free splits, and I report null results when the data shows no effect. I'm also a Graduate Teaching Assistant at IIIT Bhopal and GATE-qualified (2025, 2026).

  1. Appl. Intell.
    Adaptive multi-metric aggregation for cost-efficient and fair blockchain-federated learning
    Ripunjay Sharma, Shabd Sahay, Yatendra Sahu, Gagan Vishwakarma, and Arpita Bhargava
    Applied Intelligence, Springer, 2026
    Abs DOI

    A blockchain-federated learning framework that jointly optimizes accuracy, F1-score and weight dampening on fraud-detection data. A two-stage aggregation pipeline combines Z-score filtering with Mahalanobis-distance outlier detection and weight damping, replicated across clients in a hybrid architecture. Under a stable heterogeneous client distribution it reaches an F1-score of 0.828 (±0.018) and an AUC of 0.952 (±0.009).

  2. Under review
    FedVC: Version-controlled governance of client contributions in federated learning
    Shabd Sahay, Gagan Vishwakarma, Ripunjay Sharma, Bhupendra Singh Kirar, and Yatendra Sahu
    Under review, 2026
    Abs

    In federated learning the server never sees how a client produced its update, so dishonest clients can poison the shared model. FedVC is a governance layer that keeps an unmodified FedAvg server inside a Git-style repository: trusted aggregates are committed to a main branch, each suspected client gets a side branch, the best commit is restored at fixed intervals, and isolated clients receive a verdict at the end of every cycle. With 100 clients, 30 of them label-flippers, on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and EMNIST-Balanced, poisoned clients held 0.144–0.227 of the aggregation weight under nine baselines and 0.009–0.022 under FedVC. FedVC kept them out of 83–89% of rounds, banned all of them on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST without banning an ordinary honest client, matched undefended FedAvg's final and worst-round MCC, and uploaded 18–24% less data.
