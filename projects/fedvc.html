FedVC

Version-controlled governance of client contributions in federated learning

Dec 2025 – present · under review
Shabd Sahay, Gagan Vishwakarma, Ripunjay Sharma, Bhupendra Singh Kirar, Yatendra Sahu
Federated Learning · Byzantine-Robust Aggregation · Anomaly Detection

what I did

  • Built a governance layer that keeps an unmodified FedAvg server inside a Git-style repository: trusted aggregates are committed to main, each suspected client gets a side branch, the best commit is restored at fixed intervals, and isolated clients receive a verdict every cycle.
  • Four modules: an adaptive commit gate, graduated trust zoning, a checkpoint restoration scheduler and a verdict classifier.
  • With 100 clients (30 label-flippers) on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST and EMNIST-Balanced, cut the poisoned share of aggregation weight from 0.144–0.227 under nine baselines (including Krum, Multi-Krum, FLTrust and FoolsGold) to 0.009–0.022: a 10–16× reduction.
  • Kept attackers out of 83–89% of rounds where every baseline admitted them in all rounds, and banned all of them on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST without banning an ordinary honest client, while matching undefended FedAvg's MCC and uploading 18–24% less data.
  • Measured and reported the failure cases: under strongly non-IID data (Dirichlet α = 0.1) the poisoned share stayed at 1.5–2.9% vs 15–22% for FedAvg, but many honest clients were also banned; sign-flip attackers were contained rather than banned.
